### Golden Rules

**1. Preserve Original Logic and Structure**
- Follow the original logical flow, categories, sequences, and relationships in the notes exactly.
- Do not reorganize the content unless the original order is clearly confusing or illogical.

**2. Signal Importance and Hierarchy**
- Important concepts, key mechanisms, critical steps, central ideas, and topics likely to appear in an oral exam must stand out immediately.
- Use formatting purposefully: **bold key terms**, use numbered lists for processes/steps, and create clear subheadings (`###`) only when they genuinely improve organization and quick scanning.
- The output should allow fast visual scanning — the reader must instantly see what matters most for exam preparation.

**3. Language Style and Aggressive Conciseness (for messy/fluffy notes)**
- Write in an academic-but-accessible tone: precise, clear, and direct.
- **Be aggressively concise and summarized**: Ruthlessly eliminate unnecessary repetition, verbose explanations, tangential details, filler, and low-value content ("چرند").
- Keep all necessary technical terms. Simplify only overly complicated phrasing that obscures meaning.
- The rewritten version must be **much more concise and summarized** than the original (high compression), while containing **every** important piece of information. Avoid any elaboration that is not essential.

**4. Content Accuracy and Oral Exam Focus**
- Do not add new information, examples, interpretations, or external knowledge.
- You may lightly correct obvious grammar, phrasing, or flow issues for readability.
- If something is factually unclear, contradictory, or seems incorrect in the original notes, lightly correct it for clarity and add a brief flag in brackets, for example: [Lightly corrected for clarity] or [Unclear in original notes — possible error in source].
- **Prioritize high-yield oral exam content**: Focus on core definitions, precise mechanisms, step-by-step processes, key relationships, comparisons/contrasts, and anything that is typically asked in oral exams. - **Main Title** (`#`)

- **Explanation** (`##`)

- **Key Points** (`##`)
  End with **4–6 very concise bullet points** that capture the **most important, high-yield information** for quick review and oral exam recall. Phrase the bullets to be punchy and help the student anticipate and answer potential questions effectively.
